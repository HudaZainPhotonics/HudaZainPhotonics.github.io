## The research problem that interests me

I have spent the past several years working across different parts of the photonic sensing problem, from sensing structures and optical interrogation to experimental validation, signal processing, and increasingly machine-learning-assisted interpretation.

What interests me now is bringing those pieces together.

A sensor can show excellent sensitivity in a controlled experiment and still become difficult to use when temperature, vibration, alignment changes, environmental variation, or operational noise are introduced. In many applications, developing the sensing element is only part of the problem. We also need to think about how the signal is measured, processed, interpreted, and validated.

That leads to the central question I want to build my research around:

> How can we develop photonic sensing systems that remain accurate, reliable, and informative when they move from controlled laboratory demonstrations into complex real-world environments?

This is the progression I want to explore in my research: moving from an interesting sensing response toward a complete measurement system that can be tested, understood, and eventually used in realistic environments.

## What I bring to this direction

My background gives me a useful starting point for building this kind of work. I have worked experimentally with fiber-optic sensors, resonators and plasmonic sensors, optical interrogation systems, and distributed acoustic sensing, while also developing experience in signal processing and machine-learning-assisted analysis.

I do not see these as separate research tracks. The opportunity I see is in connecting them, and in bringing together students and collaborators with complementary expertise when a problem extends beyond any one area.

My current projects, publications, and laboratory capabilities provide a foundation for doing that while continuing to expand into new sensing problems and applications.

## What I want to build next

**Intelligent distributed fiber sensing**

I want to explore how distributed fiber measurements can become more informative in complex acoustic and vibration environments, particularly when conventional threshold-based approaches begin to struggle with noise, nuisance events, or changing operating conditions.

**Robust multi-parameter sensing**

I am interested in sensing systems that can distinguish between competing physical and environmental effects rather than treating every change as the signal of interest. This includes sensing architectures, compensation strategies, and data-driven approaches for separating multiple parameters.

**Automated optical interrogation and validation**

I also want to make the experiments themselves smarter by connecting optical hardware, automated data acquisition, calibration, and computational analysis. The goal is to make measurements easier to repeat, compare, and systematically validate under realistic environmental, mechanical, and operational conditions.

## Where I want this to go

Over the longer term, I want to build an interdisciplinary research program around intelligent photonic sensing systems for real-world monitoring.

I see this as a space where photonics, instrumentation, signal processing, machine learning, and application-specific expertise can benefit from being developed together. The applications may vary, from infrastructure and energy to environmental and health-related sensing, but the underlying challenge is similar: making reliable and useful measurements from complex physical environments.

For me, the goal is no longer simply to make a sensor more sensitive. It is to build the people, tools, experiments, and system-level understanding needed to make photonic sensing genuinely useful outside the lab.
