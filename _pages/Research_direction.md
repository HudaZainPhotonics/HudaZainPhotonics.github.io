## Central research problem

I develop photonic sensing systems because the most useful measurements are rarely made under ideal laboratory conditions.

A sensor may show high sensitivity in a controlled experiment, yet become difficult to interpret when temperature, vibration, alignment changes, environmental variation, or operational noise are introduced. My research aims to bridge this gap by connecting photonic sensing devices with robust optical interrogation, signal processing, and machine-learning-assisted interpretation.

So, my work is guided by one central question:

> How can photonic sensing systems remain accurate, reliable, and informative when they move from controlled laboratory demonstrations into complex real-world environments?

## Research logic

My research progresses from individual sensing structures toward validated systems that can support realistic measurement needs:

Photonic devices  
Fiber-optic sensors, resonators, plasmonic structures, and functional sensing platforms

↓  

Optical interrogation  
Spectral measurement, fiber-optic instrumentation, and automated data acquisition

↓  

Signal processing  
Feature extraction, calibration, noise reduction, and spectral analysis

↓  

Intelligent interpretation  
Machine-learning-assisted sensing, classification, regression, and sensor fusion

↓  

Robust sensing systems  
Environmental validation, distributed sensing, repeatability, and deployment-oriented testing

This progression reflects a broader goal: moving from sensors that demonstrate performance under controlled conditions toward sensing systems that can operate reliably in realistic environments.

## Research foundation
This roadmap builds on my work in fiber-optic sensing, resonator and plasmonic sensing, optical interrogation, distributed acoustic sensing, and machine-learning-assisted signal analysis.

My research projects, publications, and technical capabilities provide the foundation for extending these areas toward increasingly integrated and deployment-oriented sensing systems.

## Near-term research directions

**Intelligent distributed fiber sensing**

Developing data-driven methods for extracting meaningful information from distributed fiber-optic measurements, particularly in complex acoustic and vibration environments.

**Robust multi-parameter sensing**

Investigating sensing architectures and compensation strategies that can separate multiple environmental or physical parameters under changing operating conditions.

**Automated optical interrogation and validation**

Integrating optical hardware, automated data acquisition, and computational analysis to improve measurement repeatability and reduce manual intervention. This includes systematic validation under realistic environmental, mechanical, and operational conditions.

## Long-term direction

My long-term goal is to establish interdisciplinary research projects focused on intelligent photonic sensing systems for real-world monitoring.

These projects will connect photonic devices, optical interrogation, signal processing, machine learning, and distributed sensing to support reliable measurement and informed decision-making in infrastructure, energy, environmental, and health-related applications.

The objective is not simply to develop more sensitive sensors, but to develop sensing systems that can interpret complex physical environments reliably and autonomously.
