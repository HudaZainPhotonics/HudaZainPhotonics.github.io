## Overview

This project explored the development of a compact and cost-effective fiber Bragg grating (FBG) interrogator for patient-assessment applications.

The work combined optical-fiber sensing, interrogation-system design, and prototype development to support the translation of FBG-based sensing into a practical biomedical measurement platform.


## My Role

I supervised the Master’s research student on the photonics aspects of the project. My contribution included guidance on laboratory work and optical-safety practice, optical-component selection, fiber-optic sensing and interrogation-system design, prototype development, experimental planning, and technical interpretation of the results.

## Collaboration

This work was conducted in collaboration with the Biomedical Engineering Department at King Fahd University of Petroleum and Minerals (KFUPM).

## Research Significance

The project demonstrates how compact optical-fiber interrogation systems can contribute to accessible biomedical sensing and patient-assessment technologies. It also supports the broader development of practical fiber-optic sensing platforms that combine sensing performance with cost, size, and usability considerations.
